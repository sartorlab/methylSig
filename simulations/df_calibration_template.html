Degrees of freedom for the t approximation in diff_methylsig()

diff_methylsig() compares its likelihood ratio statistic to a squared t distribution. The paper sets the degrees of freedom to the number of samples minus 2, and since 2015 the package has added 2 to that. This report measures the false-positive rate of both choices, and of the χ2 approximation, on simulated data with no difference between groups.

Raymond Cavalcante 26 September 2026 methylSig 1.25.10 (Bioconductor devel)

Summary

Site by site (local_window_size = 0, the default), with overdispersed loci as the case that matters most:

  • The paper’s df is conservative at small samples and close to nominal from about 6 per group. At p < 0.05, 0.28% of overdispersed loci are false positives at 2 per group, 2.3% at 3, 4.1% at 4, and 4.8% to 5.2% from 6 to 10.
  • Adding 2 fixes the small samples and overshoots a little from 4 per group. The same rates are 3.3% at 2 per group and 4.6% at 3, then 5.2% to 5.6% from 4 to 10. At p < 0.001, both choices stay at or below the nominal 0.1%.
  • Over all loci, both are conservative, because 64% of loci have binomial variation: 0.11% (paper) and 1.6% (+ 2) at 2 per group, 3.7% and 3.9% at 10.
  • χ2 is anti-conservative everywhere: 7.2% to 11.6% on overdispersed loci at p < 0.05, and 4 to 15 times the nominal rate at p < 0.001. This supports keeping t_approx = TRUE as the default.
  • Local dispersion alone is anti-conservative here on overdispersed loci, with either df: 6.1% to 8.1% from 3 per group. The true dispersion in this simulation isn’t smooth along the genome (see Caveats), which works against local dispersion. With local dispersion and methylation together, the rates are 3.7% to 5.2% from 3 per group.

For discussion: the + 2 does what it was added for at 2 to 3 samples per group. From about 4 per group, the paper’s df is better calibrated on overdispersed loci. A correction that shrinks with sample size would keep both, and would be simple to test with this simulation.

Background

For each locus i, diff_methylsig() estimates the dispersion θ̂i, fits a methylation level for each group and one common level, and computes the likelihood ratio statistic D = 2{ℓ(μ̂ig, θ̂i) − ℓ(μ̂i, θ̂i)}. Park et al. (2014, section 2.3) approximate its distribution by D ~ tp2 with

p = Σk∈S H(k − i) (Jk1 + Jk2 − 2) Jkg: samples in group g with coverage at locus k

where S is the local window and H the weight kernel. Without local information, S is the locus alone and p = J1 + J2 − 2. In September 2015 (commit 5896c23), after discussion with Maureen Sartor, methylSig began using p + 2, because the t approximation was too conservative with small samples. In the paper’s permutation study (Fig. 2, 21 AML ERRBS samples), p degrees of freedom are conservative at 4 to 5 covered samples and slightly anti-conservative from about 7.

methylSig 1.25.7 fixed a typo in the log likelihood used with local information for methylation (local_meth = TRUE): the term for unmethylated reads lacked θ, which inflated D. With mixed cancer and normal labels on real data, 22.3% of loci had FDR < 0.05 before the fix and none after. All results here use 1.25.10, which includes the fix.

Simulation design

The null data are resampled from real data, so methylation levels, dispersion, coverage, missingness and CpG spacing are realistic, and the two groups have the same true methylation at every locus.

Loci
The first 250,000 CpGs of bsseqData’s BS.cancer.ex (chr21, 3 colon cancer and 3 normal samples), kept if they have coverage between 5 and 500 in at least 2 samples per group and could be tested on the real data: 79,735 loci.
True methylation
For each locus, the methylation of all 6 real samples pooled, the same in both simulated groups. 7% of loci are at 0 or 1, and 73% are between 0.1 and 0.9.
True dispersion
diff_methylsig()’s estimate θ̂ on the real data (cancer against normal, no local information), which is the dispersion around the group means. 64% of loci are at the upper bound θ = 106, which is effectively binomial. The other 36%, the overdispersed loci, have median θ = 10 (ρ = 1/(1 + θ) ≈ 0.09), with 10th to 90th percentiles of 2.4 and 76.
Samples
2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 10 per group, in two equal groups. Each simulated sample copies the coverage of a randomly chosen real sample at every locus, which keeps real coverage and missingness.
Reads
Methylated reads are beta-binomial: M ~ Binomial(coverage, P), with P ~ Beta(μθ, (1 − μ)θ), the model diff_methylsig() assumes.
Filtering
As a user would, loci need at least 2 covered samples per group (filter_loci_by_group_coverage()). diff_methylsig() drops loci with p ≤ 1.
Local information
None; local dispersion only (local_window_size = 200, local_meth = FALSE); or local dispersion and methylation (both TRUE, the defaults once a window is set). The default tri-weight kernel H(u) = (1 − u2)3, with up to 5 neighbors on either side.
Tests
From the same D at each locus: tp2 (the paper), tp+22 (methylSig since 2015), and χ2 with 1 df (t_approx = FALSE).
Replication
One simulated data set per sample size, tested with all three local settings. Seed 20260926.

Caveats

  1. The null follows the model. Reads are drawn from the beta-binomial distribution that diff_methylsig() fits, so this tests the t approximation, not model misspecification. The paper’s permutations of real samples include variation the model doesn’t, which may be why it found slight anti-conservatism from about 7 samples.
  2. The true dispersion is noisy and not spatially smooth. It comes from per-locus estimates on 3 against 3 samples: 64% are at the binomial bound, the Spearman correlation of log θ between adjacent loci is 0.09, and 59% of the neighbors of an overdispersed locus are binomial. Real dispersion is correlated over 200 to 300 bp (the paper’s Supplementary Figure S2), so the local-dispersion results here are a stress test, and are probably worse than on real data.
  3. Loci aren’t independent. Local windows share reads and simulated samples share coverage patterns, so rates vary more than the binomial standard error suggests (about 0.09 percentage points at 5% with 60,000 loci, 0.13 with 28,000). Differences below about 0.3 points aren’t meaningful.
  4. One data set. One region of one tissue (chr21, colon), balanced groups, and one simulated data set per sample size.
  5. Only false positives. Power isn’t measured. The + 2 raises power most at small samples, where the paper’s df is most conservative.

Reproducing

From the repository root, in the methylSig devel Docker image (docker/Dockerfile) with methylSig installed. The simulation takes about 15 minutes with 4 cores. Keep N_CORES at 4 or less in an 8 GB Docker VM: each mclapply() worker can end up copying the parent’s memory.
